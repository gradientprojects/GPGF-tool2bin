GPGF-tool2bin

Photo of a tool → Gridfinity bin STEP. Everything runs in your browser — photos never leave your device.

What goes over the network?
  • Downloads only, all of them the app itself: the page, its code, the vision and CAD engines, the blank template image the self-test checks, and the font for the revision label on the bin. After the first visit they’re cached and the app works offline.
  • Nothing is sent from your device. Opening a photo or STEP file just lets this page read it inside your browser; all processing happens on your computer, and the STEP you download is built right here. There is no server to receive anything — no account, no cookies, no analytics or tracking.
  • Your filename prefix, paper size and magnet size are kept in this browser only.
  • The files are hosted on GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, sees that the pages were requested (your IP address and browser) — never your photos or files.

Made for desktop — take the photo with your phone, then open this page on your computer to build and download the bin.

1 · Grab a sheet of paper (template optional)

  • Any plain letter or A4 sheet works as the backdrop — no printing needed.
  • The printed template is worth it when: light is uneven or shadowed (“paper sheet not found”), the sheet blends into a white/light table, or you want the best accuracy — its marker border corrects perspective far better than four paper corners, and its 100 mm ruler verifies the print scale.
  • If you print it: 100% scale, tool goes in the gray field.

2 · Scan a tool

  • Lay the tool on the sheet and photograph it with any camera (a phone is fine), then open the photo here — or drag it anywhere on this page.
  • An exported STEP goes in the same way: it reopens its design for revision, no photo needed.
  • Shoot straight down over the tool.
  • Thick tool? Step back and use 2×/3× optical zoom if your camera has it — up close, it sees the tool’s sides and the outline comes out slightly oversized (sheet correction can’t fix that).
  • Even, diffuse light beats flash — flash puts glare on shiny tools. Use the flash only if it’s too dim.

3 · Pocket profile

pocket uses the tool’s convex hull — every notch, gap and concavity is bridged over, leaving one simple outline
  • the pocket is never closer to the tool than the clearance below, at every point — smoothing may only bow outward
  • off: smoothing can cut tight corners slightly closer (you’ll get a warning if it does)
  • straight tool edges stay straight (within ~0.3 mm) instead of bowing outward with high smoothness
  • corners still round per the smoothness setting
  • mirrors the pocket across the tool’s symmetry axis — evens out lighting artifacts on symmetric tools
  • turn off for genuinely asymmetric ones
pocket depth
either way the pocket ends ≥7 mm above the bin’s bottom
gap kept between the tool and the pocket wall — how loose the fit is
how much the outline is relaxed into a flowing curve — higher hides scan noise and small detail
  • finger scallops at the tool’s left and right ends so you can lift it out — 0 removes them
  • drag the blue dots in the preview to reposition them
the scallops cost a grid unit:
  • thinnest allowed material between the pocket and the bin’s outside
  • the bin grows a 42 mm cell when the pocket would get too close

4 · Bin

  • in front of every exported filename
  • remembered on this device; clear it if you don’t want one
  • pockets under all four corners of every 42 mm foot, with a 0.5 mm chamfered entry
  • these are the POCKET dimensions as cut — include your own press-fit allowance (e.g. a 6×2 mm magnet → 6.15 × 2.1)
  • limits keep the Gridfinity foot printable: ⌀ up to 6.8 mm, depth up to 3 mm (remembered on this device)
  • the revision (R01, R02, …) debossed 0.4 mm into the underside
  • reads when you flip the bin over
  • 1 mm chamfer on the rim and the pocket entry — easier tool drop-in
  • or sharp square edges
