GPGF-tool2bin

Photo of a tool → Gridfinity bin STEP. Everything runs in your browser — photos never leave your device.

1 · Grab a sheet of paper (template optional)

  • Any plain letter or A4 sheet works as the backdrop — no printing needed.
  • The printed template is worth it when: light is uneven or shadowed (“paper sheet not found”), the sheet blends into a white/light table, or you want the best accuracy — its marker border corrects perspective far better than four paper corners, and its 100 mm ruler verifies the print scale.
  • If you print it: 100% scale, tool goes in the gray field.
🖨 Letter template  ·  🖨 A4 template

2 · Scan a tool

  • Lay the tool on the sheet; on a phone the button opens the camera. You can also drag a photo anywhere on this page.
  • Shoot straight down over the tool.
  • Thick tool? Step back and use 2×/3× optical zoom if your phone has it — up close, the camera sees the tool’s sides and the outline comes out slightly oversized (sheet correction can’t fix that).
  • Even, diffuse light beats flash — flash puts glare on shiny tools. Your camera’s own flash toggle is there if it’s too dim.
sets the mm scale when you shoot on a plain sheet; ignored when the template is detected
scans at 20 px/mm instead of 12 — roughly 3× slower; the default already out-resolves what a printed bin can reproduce

3 · Pocket profile

pocket uses the tool’s convex hull — every notch, gap and concavity is bridged over, leaving one simple outline
the pocket is never closer to the tool than the clearance below, at every point — smoothing may only bow outward. Off: smoothing can cut tight corners slightly closer (you’ll get a warning if it does)
mirrors the pocket across the tool’s symmetry axis — evens out lighting artifacts on symmetric tools; turn off for genuinely asymmetric ones
gap kept between the tool and the pocket wall — how loose the fit is
how much the outline is relaxed into a flowing curve — higher hides scan noise and small detail
finger scallops at the tool’s left and right ends (blue dots in the preview) so you can lift it out — 0 removes them
thinnest allowed material between the pocket and the bin’s outside — the bin grows a 42 mm cell when the pocket would get too close

4 · Bin

part name — goes in the filename and the STEP’s part name
in front of every exported filename (remembered on this device; clear it if you don’t want one)
sets the pocket depth; bin height rounds up in 7 mm Gridfinity units and always keeps a ≥5 mm floor (very thick tools sit a little proud)
pockets for 6×2 mm magnets under all four corners of every 42 mm foot, with a chamfered press-fit entry
the revision (R01, R02, …) debossed 0.4 mm into the underside — reads when you flip the bin over
1 mm chamfer on the rim and the pocket entry (easier tool drop-in), or sharp square edges

The STEP carries its own design: drop an exported .step back onto this page to revise it (no photo needed) — the next export uprevs itself (R01 → R02).
